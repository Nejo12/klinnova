## 3. Berlin population and international target-market context

### 3.1 Population

Amt für Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg reported an official Berlin population of **3,700,577 at 31 December 2025** under the population-projection series based on Census 2022.

Of these:

- **2,867,364** held German citizenship;
- **833,213** were foreign citizens;
- foreign citizens therefore represented about **22.5%** of the official population.

The same authority's population-register series reports **3,913,644 registered residents** at the end of 2025, of whom **976,419** were foreign citizens. The two totals are methodologically different and must not be mixed in one calculation.

Source:
- https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/presse/2026/73-bevoelkerungsfortschreibung-2025-berlin/
- https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/meine-region/berlin-statistik/einwohnerbestand/

### 3.2 Migration-background context

The Berlin population-register statistics reported that **41.7%** of Berlin residents had a migration background at 30 June 2025, comprising foreign citizens plus German citizens with a migration background.

This is relevant as broad context, but it is **not** Klinnova's addressable candidate market by itself. Migration background does not imply limited German, unemployment, job search, work authorization, or interest in Klinnova's initial roles.

Source:
- https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/132-2025/

### 3.3 Businessplan implication

A defensible statement is:

> Berlin is a highly international labour market with a large foreign and migration-background population, making language accessibility and labour-market integration commercially relevant topics.

Do **not** convert the 22.5% foreign-citizen share or 41.7% migration-background share directly into Klinnova TAM.

## 4. Berlin labour-market context

### 4.1 Overall labour market

Bundesagentur für Arbeit reported for **July 2026**:

- Berlin unemployment rate: **10.4%**;
- rate one month earlier: 10.2%;
- labour demand in Berlin increased during the month despite the weak overall market.

For **2025 as a whole**, BA reported:

- average Berlin unemployment: about **218,000 people**;
- average unemployment rate: **10.3%**;
- continued pressure from weak economic conditions;
- employment broadly stable, but employers cautious in hiring.

Sources:
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/rd-bb/presse/2026-17-berlinbrandenburg-schwacher-arbeitsmarkt-schlagt-auf-ausbildungsmarkt-durch
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/rd-bb/presse/2026-4-jahresruckblick-2025

### 4.2 Employment base

BA's Arbeitsmarktmonitor reports for Berlin in 2025:

- **1,682,946** socially insured employees;
- employment rate (svB): **59.2%**;
- unemployment rate: **10.3%**;
- underemployment rate: **12.2%**;
- tertiary-sector share: **87.5%**;
- part-time share: **35.1%**.

Source:
- https://arbeitsmarktmonitor.arbeitsagentur.de/faktencheck/regionalstruktur/tabelle/532/2025/employrate/

### 4.3 Businessplan implication

Klinnova should not claim that Berlin lacks jobseekers. The market evidence instead supports a **matching/activation problem**:

- meaningful unemployment and underemployment coexist with employer demand;
- the challenge is to connect suitable jobseekers to legitimate roles with fewer avoidable screening barriers.

## 5. Language as a labour-market barrier

### 5.1 IAB evidence

IAB's 2026 research on German-language requirements reports that **about half of establishments require at least B2 German when hiring**.

IAB also states that many immigrants arrive with limited German and seek employment before reaching higher language proficiency. Employers can, however, consider candidates with lower German levels where the role allows it or where language development is ongoing.

Source:
- https://iab.de/deutschkenntnisse-und-arbeitsmarktintegration/
- IAB Kurzbericht 7/2026: https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2026/kb2026-07.pdf

### 5.2 Refugee/integration evidence

BA's 2025 regional review states that missing qualifications and language skills continue to lengthen labour-market integration pathways, even as employment integration improves.

IAB's longer-term refugee research also identifies German-language acquisition as one of the factors that materially improves employment integration.

Sources:
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/rd-bb/presse/2026-4-jahresruckblick-2025
- https://iab.de/presseinfo/10-jahre-fluchtmigration-beschaeftigungsquote-von-gefluechteten-naehert-sich-dem-durchschnitt-in-deutschland-an/

### 5.3 Businessplan implication

This directly supports Klinnova's core design thesis:

> Language proficiency should be treated as a structured job-fit variable, not as a blanket proxy for employability.

The application should avoid claiming that employers generally discriminate by language. The evidence supports that many roles require substantial German, while some roles can legitimately accept lower levels.

## 6. Foreign labour as an economic necessity

BA reported in July 2026 that German employment growth has been carried by foreign workers since 2024, while demographic ageing reduces the domestic labour-force base. BA explicitly frames labour migration as important for maintaining labour supply.

Source:
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/presse/2026-25-beschaeftigungswachstum-wird-von-auslaendischen-arbeitnehmern-getragen

Businessplan implication:

Klinnova's target group should not be framed primarily as a social-welfare population. A stronger economic framing is that international labour is increasingly material to labour-force stability, while language and conventional recruitment processes can slow matching.

## 7. Cleaning / facility-services entry wedge

### 7.1 Berlin employer landscape

Amt für Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg's 2023 enterprise-register statistics show a substantial Berlin cleaning/facility-services base.

Published figures include:

- WZ 81 — building services / gardening: **4,566 legal units** in the relevant structural-statistics dataset;
- WZ 81.2 — cleaning of buildings, streets and transport: **2,412 units**;
- WZ 81.21 — general building cleaning: **1,870 units**.

A separate enterprise-register publication reports, among subcategories:

- WZ 81.22 special building/machine cleaning: **344 legal units**;
- WZ 81.29 other cleaning: **266 legal units**.

These publications use specific statistical concepts and reference years. Figures should not be summed across incompatible tables without checking definitions.

Sources:
- https://download.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/0c5ab317b34c8622/a009739fd3e2/SB_L04-01-00_2023j01_BE.pdf
- https://download.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/1d1de24751e082a0/4d6df7bf7311/SB_D02-01-00_2023j01_BE.pdf

### 7.2 Current vacancy signal

A BA Jobsuche query for Berlin showed, at research time:

- about **209** results for `Gebäudereinigung`;
- about **72** results for `Reinigungskraft Gebäudereinigung`.

This is a live search snapshot, not a measure of total market demand, because:

- one vacancy can appear under multiple searches;
- BA does not contain every vacancy in Berlin;
- counts change continuously;
- some results are supervisory or specialist roles rather than entry-level cleaning work.

Sources:
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/jobsuche/suche?angebotsart=1&arbeitsort=Berlin&was=Geb%C3%A4udereinigung
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/jobsuche/suche?angebotsart=1&arbeitsort=Berlin&was=Reinigungskraft+Geb%C3%A4udereinigung

### 7.3 Why cleaning remains a defensible beachhead

The evidence supports cleaning as a **large enough, fragmented Berlin employer segment** with recurring hiring activity.

It does **not** yet prove a sector-wide labour shortage specific to entry-level cleaners in Berlin.

Therefore the Businessplan wording should be:

> Cleaning/facility services are selected as Klinnova's initial market-entry wedge because Berlin has a sizeable fragmented employer base, visible recurring vacancy activity, structured job requirements and roles where lower German proficiency can sometimes be compatible with the work.

Do not claim a quantified cleaning-worker shortage until stronger occupation-specific Berlin evidence is obtained.

## 8. Competitive landscape

### 8.1 Bundesagentur für Arbeit Jobsuche

**Model:** public employment service / broad job marketplace.

Relevant features:

- one of Germany's largest online job boards;
- employers can publish jobs free of charge;
- jobs can be managed directly or with Arbeitgeber-Service support;
- registered candidates can maintain profiles and application documents;
- employer-side tools include vacancy management and candidate/proposal workflows.

Sources:
- https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/bewerbungstraining/job-finden
- https://web.arbeitsagentur.de/vermittlung/ag-stea-ui/pc/stellenmeldung

**Klinnova implication:** Klinnova cannot compete on price alone because BA already offers free posting. Differentiation must come from target-group fit, structured language matching, simplicity and founder-assisted validation.

### 8.2 Indeed

**Model:** broad commercial job marketplace.

Candidate-side evidence shows Indeed applications commonly use an Indeed CV or uploaded CV and can include employer screening questions. Employers receiving an application can access CV and qualification information submitted by the candidate.

Sources:
- https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/204652920-auf-indeed-auf-einen-job-bewerben?co=DE&hl=de
- https://www.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/360051197532-profil-und-pr%C3%A4ferenzen-was-wird-an-arbeitgeber-weitergegeben?co=DE&hl=de

**Klinnova implication:** Klinnova's non-CV-first model and explicit language/availability/location eligibility are meaningful differentiators for the initial target group, but Indeed's reach is far greater.

### 8.3 Stepstone

**Model:** broad paid recruiting marketplace / recruiting suite.

Current official employer pages report:

- more than **11.8 million registered users**;
- more than **4 million profiles** in the CV database;
- matching, candidate recommendations, screening and applicant-management tooling;
- pricing ranging from annual small-business packages to individual paid ads; current public pricing includes a small-business package from **EUR 249/month for 12 months** and individual Pro Ads from **EUR 1,449 per listing**.

Sources:
- https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/
- https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/stellenanzeige-aufgeben/
- https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/produkte/recruit/

**Klinnova implication:** Stepstone is a reach/recruiting-technology competitor, not a narrow language-access product. Klinnova should not attempt to match Stepstone's ATS/recruiting breadth in the beta.

### 8.4 Kleinanzeigen Jobs

**Model:** broad classified marketplace with a jobs category.

Official/public marketplace evidence shows active job listings across many low- and mid-skill categories, including cleaning, mini-jobs and side jobs.

Source:
- https://www.kleinanzeigen.de/s-stellenanzeigen/k0

**Klinnova implication:** Kleinanzeigen is especially important because it competes for simple local hiring demand with low posting friction. Klinnova therefore needs to provide more value than merely publishing a local vacancy: structured qualification, safer employer verification, candidate fit and application outcome tracking.

### 8.5 Zenjob

**Model:** digital staffing / temporary-work platform, especially for students and flexible shifts.

Zenjob states that:

- employers can book flexible workers digitally;
- Berlin is a major operating market;
- its platform covers logistics, hospitality, retail and other flexible roles;
- jobseekers can often work without a CV or conventional application process;
- the worker relationship is materially different from Klinnova: Zenjob handles personnel administration and workers sign employment contracts for assignments; student eligibility is a major part of the model.

Sources:
- https://www.zenjob.com/de/personal/region/berlin/
- https://www.zenjob.com/de/jobs/
- https://www.zenjob.com/de/

**Klinnova implication:** Zenjob proves demand for low-friction app-based job access, but Klinnova's model is not temporary staffing/employee leasing and is not restricted to student flexible-work arrangements.

### 8.6 Helpling

**Model:** service marketplace for household/commercial cleaning and related services.

Helpling's current terms/help pages state that:

- it connects customers with independent service providers or partner-network workers;
- independent cleaners are not employees of the customer or Helpling under the self-employed-provider model;
- service providers undergo identity verification;
- providers may need their own business registration and other self-employment data;
- Berlin customers compare provider profiles, prices and reviews and book cleaning services through the platform.

Sources:
- https://www.helpling.de/agb-reinigungskraft/
- https://help.helpling.de/de/articles/250682-bin-ich-der-arbeitgeber-der-reinigungskraft
- https://www.helpling.de/reinigungskraft/berlin/

**Klinnova implication:** Helpling is not a normal employment marketplace. It primarily mediates cleaning services, often through self-employed providers. Klinnova explicitly targets legitimate employment opportunities and excludes freelancer-marketplace operations in its current product boundary. This distinction should be clear in the Businessplan.

## 10. What this research changes in the innovation argument

The initial innovation thesis survives competitor review, but it should be sharpened.

Weak claim:

> Klinnova is innovative because people can find jobs without a CV.

This is not sufficient: Zenjob already demonstrates a no-CV / low-friction path, and general platforms increasingly simplify applications.

Stronger claim:

> Klinnova combines structured language-level accessibility, practical eligibility matching, non-CV-first application, employer verification, local low-language-barrier job focus and measurable employment outcomes in one Berlin-focused employment marketplace.

The innovation is therefore the **integrated workflow and target-market design**, not any single feature.

## 11. Preliminary TAM / SAM / SOM methodology

Do not manufacture a market size by multiplying Berlin population by a guessed percentage.

### TAM — long-term conceptual market

Long-term potential could include Berlin employers with roles where practical job fit can be evaluated through structured language, location, availability and employment requirements, plus work-authorized candidates seeking such roles.

This is broader than cleaning and should not be quantified until sector/employer data is assembled across the intended long-term categories.

### SAM — initial serviceable market

The initial SAM should be bounded to:

- Berlin;
- cleaning/facility/premises-support employers plus at most tightly adjacent low-language-barrier roles;
- candidates legally allowed to work who are interested in these roles and for whom German-language barriers are materially relevant.

Employer-side official statistics indicate **at least a four-figure Berlin cleaning-business universe**, but the exact serviceable subset requires filtering for business size, hiring frequency and compatibility with Klinnova's employment model.

### SOM — first funded validation period

For the initial validation period, use the operational cohort model rather than an inflated percentage-of-market claim:

- Cohort A: 3–5 employers, 10–20 vacancies, 20–30 candidates;
- Cohort B: 8–10 employers, 30–50 vacancies, 60–100 candidates;
- Cohort C: 20 employers, 100 vacancies, 200 candidates.

These are evidence-generation targets, not market-share forecasts.

## 12. Businessplan claims now supported

The following claims are now reasonably supportable with primary evidence:

1. Berlin is a highly international city with a large foreign/migration-background population.
2. Berlin has material unemployment/underemployment while employers continue to report vacancies.
3. German-language requirements are a real labour-market integration variable; many establishments expect B2 or higher.
4. Foreign workers are increasingly important to Germany's employment base.
5. Berlin has a substantial and fragmented cleaning/facility-services business population.
6. Cleaning vacancies are visibly active in the BA job market.
7. Klinnova faces strong substitutes, including free public job posting and very large commercial platforms.
8. Therefore Klinnova cannot credibly differentiate on 'jobs online' or 'free posting' alone.
9. The strongest differentiation is the integrated language-access + practical-fit + trust + outcome workflow for a narrowly defined Berlin segment.

## 13. Claims not yet supported strongly enough

Do not yet state as facts:

- Berlin has a quantified shortage of X cleaning workers;
- X% of foreign Berlin residents have limited German;
- X% of limited-German jobseekers cannot find work because of language;
- Klinnova can acquire employers/candidates at a specific CAC;
- Klinnova will achieve a specific conversion/hire rate;
- Klinnova's TAM equals the foreign population or migration-background population;
- employers prefer Klinnova to Indeed, BA, Kleinanzeigen or Stepstone;
- Klinnova has proven product-market fit.

These require further evidence or real post-application market validation.

## 15. F2A verdict

**F2A first evidence pass: COMPLETE / usable for Businessplan drafting.**

The research materially strengthens the application case but also narrows the claims we should make.

Most important conclusion:

> Klinnova should not present itself as a cheaper or simpler generic job board. Free and high-reach substitutes already exist. Its defensible market-entry thesis is a focused Berlin employment-access layer that makes language compatibility, practical eligibility, employer trust and real outcomes explicit for roles where conventional CV/language defaults create avoidable friction.
