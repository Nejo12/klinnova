# Terminology

Consistent terminology keeps product copy, code, analytics, and support aligned.

## Primary terms

### Candidate

A person using Stint to discover and apply for work.

Use `candidate` in product, documentation, code, and analytics. Use `applicant` only after a candidate has submitted an application to a specific job.

Avoid using `worker`, `job seeker`, and `talent` interchangeably.

### Employer

A verified person or organisation publishing a vacancy and reviewing candidates.

Use `company` for the legal or public organisation profile. Use `employer` for the hiring role in the product.

### Job

The primary user-facing term for an employment opportunity.

Use `vacancy` in employer and administrative contexts when referring to an open position. Avoid mixing `role`, `opening`, `position`, and `opportunity` without purpose.

### Application

A candidate's submitted expression of interest in one job.

### Match

A job shown because it satisfies the candidate's essential preferences and eligibility criteria. A match is not a guarantee of interview or employment.

### Language requirement

The level genuinely required to perform the work and complete the hiring process.

Initial labels:

- English accepted
- No German required
- Basic German
- Conversational German
- Fluent German
- Unclear; verification required

### Verified employer

An employer whose identity and company evidence have been manually reviewed by Stint. Verification is not an endorsement of every future action.

### Verified job

A vacancy confirmed as active and reviewed for required structured information. It does not guarantee hiring or employment quality.

### Work-authorisation declaration

The candidate's self-confirmation that they may legally work in Germany. It is not document verification or legal advice.

### Step

A meaningful movement in the candidate or employer journey. Do not use the step motif for decorative progress that has no real outcome.

## Preferred copy

- Find jobs that fit your language and availability.
- Applications do not need a CV to start.
- English accepted.
- Why this job matches you.
- One step closer.
- This job is no longer available.
- This one was not the right fit. Keep moving.

## Avoid

- unskilled worker
- low-skilled labour
- foreign worker
- perfect match
- guaranteed job
- AI-powered candidate
- rejected candidate as a permanent identity
- illegal worker

Use factual, specific language rather than labels that reduce a person to a circumstance.
