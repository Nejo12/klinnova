## 2. Executive summary — working draft

Klinnova is a Berlin-based digital employment platform designed to reduce avoidable friction between employers with accessible vacancies and work-authorised jobseekers whose German level or conventional CV profile can limit visibility in mainstream recruitment processes.

The product combines structured language compatibility, practical eligibility matching, non-CV-first candidate access, verified employer participation, explainable matching and measurable interview/hire outcomes. Klinnova is not positioned as a generic job board. Its initial market-entry strategy is deliberately narrow: Berlin first, with cleaning and facility-services employers as the initial wedge because the segment is fragmented, has visible recurring vacancy activity and includes roles where practical availability and language fit can be more decisive than a conventional CV-first recruitment process.

A working software MVP already exists and was developed before the planned funding application. No historical MVP development cost is intended to be claimed. The proposed future funded project is a clearly separated market-establishment, commercialisation and evidence-driven development phase covering controlled employer/candidate acquisition, product refinement based on real beta use, selected specialist services, project-attributable IT/operating costs and controlled scaling in Berlin.

The current base funding plan is approximately **EUR 40,000 eligible project expenditure / EUR 20,000 requested grant**. A stretch corridor of approximately **EUR 45,000–50,000 eligible expenditure / EUR 22,500–25,000 requested grant** may be considered only if final quotations, eligibility confirmation and liquidity evidence justify it.

The Founder intends to remain in full-time employment during the initial Klinnova build-up. Founder remuneration is therefore EUR 0 in the base project model. The operating model is Founder-led with bounded use of external specialists rather than permanent employees at project start. This reduces burn rate and financing risk while the commercial base is validated.

## 4. Problem and customer need

### 4.1 Candidate-side problem

Klinnova targets work-authorised jobseekers in Berlin who may face avoidable friction because:

- German proficiency is limited relative to many mainstream vacancies;
- actual vacancy language requirements are often unclear;
- conventional recruitment can over-rely on CV format and keyword history;
- availability, location, shift preference and employment type may matter more than formal presentation for many local operational roles;
- candidates need clearer signals about whether a role is genuinely accessible before applying.

Current IAB evidence recorded in F2A indicates that about half of establishments require at least B2 German when hiring. This supports the relevance of language as a structured matching variable while also requiring claim discipline: many roles legitimately require substantial German and Klinnova should not imply otherwise.

### 4.2 Employer-side problem

Small and local employers may face:

- unsuitable applications;
- unclear candidate fit on practical criteria;
- recurring hiring needs without requiring a broad enterprise ATS;
- difficulty accessing under-reached candidate groups through conventional channels;
- need for a controlled, verified vacancy-to-applicant workflow.

Klinnova aims to reduce mismatch rather than merely add another vacancy publication channel.

## 6. Innovation and differentiation

Klinnova's innovation case is the integrated digital/social labour-market workflow rather than a claim of novel underlying computer-science research.

Core differentiation:

1. **Language compatibility as structured matching data** — language requirement is treated as a first-class job-fit variable.
2. **Practical eligibility before CV prestige** — location, availability, shift, employment type and actual role requirements are central.
3. **Non-CV-first access** — structured candidate profiles can reduce unnecessary CV friction where the employer does not genuinely require one.
4. **Explainable matching** — deterministic reasons for fit/non-fit rather than opaque ranking.
5. **Verification and legitimacy controls** — employer verification, vacancy moderation and trust/safety workflows are embedded in the marketplace.
6. **Outcome measurement** — applications, completed interviews and confirmed hires are measured separately rather than equating registration/activity with employment success.

The competitive evidence shows that individual elements may exist elsewhere. The claim is therefore not that competitors lack every comparable feature; the differentiation is the integrated process for the specific Berlin target group and initial market wedge.

## 9. Competition and positioning

Relevant current substitutes/competitors include:

- Bundesagentur für Arbeit Jobsuche — major free/public substitute;
- Indeed — major reach marketplace with CV/profile-heavy workflows;
- Stepstone — high-reach paid recruiting platform;
- Kleinanzeigen Jobs — low-friction local classified substitute;
- Zenjob — adjacent low-friction digital staffing model;
- Helpling — adjacent cleaning-service marketplace with a materially different self-employment/intermediary model.

Commercial implication:

> Klinnova cannot defend itself merely by being online, free, local or easy to post on. It must monetise differentiated employer value such as candidate relevance, reduced mismatch, structured workflow, verified participation, targeted reach and recurring hiring efficiency.
