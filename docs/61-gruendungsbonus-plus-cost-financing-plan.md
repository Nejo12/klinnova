## 3. Current official programme rules relevant to F4

Current official IBB/IBT material states, subject to the full guideline and the individual funding decision:

- grant intensity: 50% of eligible expenditure;
- maximum grant: EUR 50,000;
- eligible-cost period: generally up to two years from application receipt / within the approval period;
- candidate categories include material/investment expenditure, running operating expenditure, personnel and external services such as consulting and IT development;
- expenditure intended for funding must respect the programme's project-start / contract-timing rules;
- payout follows the reimbursement principle;
- current IBB/IBT material provides for up to four tranches, normally with each grant tranche at least 10% of the approved grant;
- with the first reimbursement request, closed financing must be demonstrated;
- payments and supporting evidence must be non-cash; cash receipts are not recognized;
- duplicate public funding for the same eligible expenditure is prohibited;
- personnel-cost limits apply, including founder/management-specific limits.

Primary current sources:

- https://www.ibb.de/de/foerderprogramme/gruendungsbonus-plus.html
- https://www.ibb-business-team.de/gruendungsbonus-plus/antragstellung-rechtliches/
- https://www.ibb.de/media/dokumente/foerderprogramme/wirtschaftsfoerderung/gruendungsbonus-plus/richtlinie-gruendungsbonus-plus.pdf

All rules must be rechecked immediately before submission and against the eventual grant decision.

## 4. Recommended project envelope

### 4.1 Base recommendation

The strongest current risk-adjusted target is:

> **approximately EUR 40,000 eligible project expenditure / approximately EUR 20,000 requested grant**

This should be the base application-planning envelope until real quotations and IBT clarifications justify a higher amount.

Why:

1. it matches the lower end of the Founder-approved EUR 20,000–25,000 grant corridor;
2. it avoids constructing an oversized project solely to maximize subsidy;
3. it requires an applicant share of approximately EUR 20,000 rather than EUR 50,000;
4. it is more compatible with EUR 5,000 starting business liquidity and EUR 300–500 monthly Founder contributions;
5. it can be staged over the two-year period and paired with reimbursement tranches;
6. the project can be expanded toward EUR 45,000–50,000 only if evidence, quotations and liquidity support it.

### 4.2 Stretch corridor

A reasonable stretch corridor is:

- total eligible costs: **EUR 45,000–50,000**;
- requested grant: **EUR 22,500–25,000**;
- applicant share: **EUR 22,500–25,000**.

Do not move to this corridor merely because it remains within the Founder-approved grant ambition. It should require a stronger cost basis and a demonstrated financing bridge.

### 4.3 Maximum programme envelope

The EUR 100,000 / EUR 50,000-grant maximum is not recommended for the current application model. It would create disproportionate pre-financing and execution risk relative to Klinnova's current operating structure.

## 7. Financing architecture — EUR 40,000 base case

### 7.1 Funding split

Illustrative 50% structure:

- eligible expenditure: EUR 40,000;
- requested grant: EUR 20,000;
- applicant share: EUR 20,000.

### 7.2 Founder-controlled funding capacity

Using the approved assumptions:

**Base 24-month Founder funding capacity before business revenue:**

- initial business liquidity: EUR 5,000;
- monthly contribution: EUR 400 × 24 = EUR 9,600;
- total planned Founder cash contribution across 24 months: **EUR 14,600**.

Sensitivity:

- low case: EUR 5,000 + EUR 300 × 24 = **EUR 12,200**;
- upper-safe case: EUR 7,500 + EUR 500 × 24 = **EUR 19,500**.

These figures are not the same as peak pre-financing capacity. Timing matters because reimbursement arrives after expenditure and evidence.

### 7.3 Residual financing requirement

In the EUR 40,000 base case, the applicant share is EUR 20,000.

Against the EUR 14,600 base Founder funding capacity, a nominal **EUR 5,400 residual gap** remains before considering Klinnova business revenue.

That gap should preferably be solved through:

1. early operating revenue once the funded market-entry phase begins and monetisation is validated;
2. cost staging / delaying non-critical expenditure;
3. reducing the final project envelope if required;
4. only if the grant is approved and reimbursement timing makes it necessary, a small short-duration liquidity bridge.

The plan must not assume that a loan exists before it actually does.

## 8. Reimbursement and peak-liquidity strategy

The reimbursement principle means the core risk is not only the 50% applicant share. Klinnova must first pay eligible invoices/costs and then request reimbursement.

### 8.1 Base operating principle

Do not spend the EUR 40,000 project budget evenly or front-load it.

Use staged expenditure blocks tied to reimbursement milestones.

For an illustrative EUR 20,000 approved grant:

- 10% minimum grant tranche = approximately EUR 2,000 grant reimbursement;
- at a 50% funding rate, that corresponds conceptually to approximately EUR 4,000 eligible expenditure before a minimum-sized reimbursement claim, subject to the actual grant rules and approved cost composition.

This is compatible with a EUR 5,000 starting business-liquidity base **only if** the first expenditure block is carefully controlled and reimbursement processing is not assumed instantaneous.

### 8.2 Recommended cash-control policy

Before each significant expenditure commitment:

1. confirm it is inside the approved funded-project scope;
2. confirm the contract/procurement timing complies with the grant decision;
3. verify sufficient cash remains to preserve ordinary Klinnova operations;
4. forecast the next reimbursement request and expected processing buffer;
5. do not use private emergency reserves to make the project appear financed;
6. stop or defer non-essential spending if the liquidity buffer falls below the approved internal floor.

### 8.3 Internal liquidity floor

Working recommendation:

> Maintain at least **EUR 2,000–3,000** of uncommitted Klinnova cash as an operating/reimbursement-delay buffer once funded execution begins.

This is an internal risk-control recommendation, not an IBB rule.

## 9. Full-time employment as financing-risk control

The Founder intends to remain in full-time employment while Klinnova establishes a stable base.

Financial implications:

- Founder personal living costs are not dependent on immediate Klinnova remuneration;
- the base project does not need to finance Founder salary;
- monthly Founder contributions can remain modest and predictable;
- the Founder does not need to leave employment merely to make the funding plan work;
- transition from employment should depend on demonstrated Klinnova commercial stability rather than the receipt of a grant.

The Businessplan should present this as disciplined staged entrepreneurship, not as lack of commitment to Klinnova.

## 11. Founder remuneration treatment

Base model:

> **EUR 0 Founder remuneration from the funded project.**

If IBT confirms that founder remuneration can be recognized for the specific sole-proprietor/Nebenerwerb structure, it may be evaluated separately. The project's financing and viability must not depend on that treatment.

Do not describe possible Founder remuneration as an "upside" or benefit of the grant.

## 12. Debt policy

Base model:

> **No debt-financed operating model.**

A borrowing facility may be considered only if all of the following are true:

1. the GründungsBONUS Plus project has been approved;
2. the underlying expenditure is justified and lies within the approved project;
3. the facility bridges reimbursement timing rather than chronic operating losses;
4. repayment remains sustainable even if reimbursement is delayed;
5. borrowing cost does not materially weaken project economics.

No debt should be represented as committed or available unless a real facility exists.

## 17. Risk controls

### Risk: grant approval lower than requested

Response:

- prioritize Priority A cost blocks;
- remove/defer discretionary spending;
- do not automatically replace missing grant with personal debt.

### Risk: reimbursement delay

Response:

- preserve liquidity floor;
- use smaller eligible expenditure blocks;
- submit reimbursement requests promptly when valid;
- bridge only when grant approval and repayment capacity make it safe.

### Risk: revenue starts later than planned

Response:

- Founder remains employed;
- Founder remuneration stays zero;
- defer optional contractors/marketing;
- keep permanent headcount at zero.

### Risk: external-service quotations exceed planning amounts

Response:

- compare providers/scope;
- reduce scope to application-critical deliverables;
- adjust project total instead of forcing the EUR 40,000 envelope.

### Risk: IBT rejects a planned cost category

Response:

- do not substitute an unrelated cost simply to preserve grant size;
- recalculate the project around genuinely necessary eligible expenditure.
