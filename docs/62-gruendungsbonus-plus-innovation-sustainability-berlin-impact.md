## 3. Core F5 position

Klinnova should not present its innovation case as "a new online job board."

The strongest defensible position is a **digital and social labour-market access innovation** that combines:

- explicit language compatibility as a first-class hiring/matching variable;
- practical eligibility matching beyond keyword search;
- non-CV-first candidate access;
- employer-side structured vacancy requirements;
- verified employer participation and vacancy-legitimacy controls;
- explainable rather than opaque matching;
- candidate-safe application/outcome visibility;
- measurable interview/hire outcome tracking;
- a controlled Berlin-first market-entry model focused initially on lower-language-barrier work.

The innovation is primarily in the integrated workflow, product design and market-access model rather than a claim of novel underlying computer-science research.

## 4. Innovation case

### 4.1 Problem with conventional recruitment flows

The target candidate may be legally permitted to work in Germany yet face avoidable friction because:

- German proficiency is limited;
- vacancies often do not make the actual language threshold explicit;
- conventional recruitment may over-rely on German-format CVs and keyword history;
- availability, travel radius, shift preferences and employment type can matter more than formal presentation for many local operational roles;
- candidates need clearer signals about whether a role is actually accessible before applying.

The employer-side problem is complementary:

- employers may receive unsuitable applications;
- a general-purpose job board does not automatically translate operational requirements into practical candidate fit;
- language requirements may be overstated or left ambiguous;
- small/local employers may not need a broad ATS but do need a controlled vacancy-to-applicant workflow.

### 4.2 Klinnova innovation components

#### A. Language compatibility as structured matching data

Klinnova treats language requirements and candidate language comfort as explicit matching inputs rather than relying only on free-text vacancy descriptions or translated interface text.

This supports the thesis that a role requiring little/no German should not be hidden from an otherwise suitable candidate merely because the candidate cannot satisfy a generic language expectation.

#### B. Practical eligibility before CV prestige

The matching model prioritises operational fit such as:

- job category;
- language requirements;
- location/travel boundary;
- availability;
- employment type;
- shift preference;
- experience requirement;
- work-authorisation declaration.

This is particularly relevant for roles where reliability, schedule fit and practical availability can be more decisive than a polished conventional CV.

#### C. Explainable matching

Klinnova's current matching direction is deterministic/explainable rather than an opaque AI score.

The product should be able to explain why a vacancy is suitable or unsuitable instead of presenting a black-box ranking.

This is important for trust, debugging, employer/candidate clarity and evidence-driven refinement during beta.

#### D. Non-CV-first application path

The current product direction permits candidates to build a structured profile and apply without making a German-format CV a mandatory universal gate.

That reduces access friction while preserving the employer's ability to specify when a CV is genuinely required.

#### E. Trust and legitimacy embedded in workflow

Employer verification, vacancy moderation, stale-vacancy controls, account suspension and audit/history mechanisms are part of the operating model rather than being treated only as after-the-fact support.

The model explicitly rejects:

- undeclared work;
- fabricated vacancies;
- unrestricted scraping as foundational supply;
- scraped candidate profiles;
- freelancer-marketplace operation in the current scope.

#### F. Outcome measurement

Klinnova measures marketplace usefulness through employment outcomes such as:

- applications;
- completed interviews;
- confirmed hires.

The current M5 semantics deliberately keep workflow status separate from verified outcome facts so, for example, an application status alone does not automatically become a claimed hire.

This supports a stronger evidence culture than measuring registrations or page views alone.

## 5. Why this is not merely feature bundling

The Businessplan should explain that the value depends on how the elements interact:

> A candidate with limited German identifies a role whose real language requirement is compatible, can understand the operational conditions, can apply through a structured profile without unnecessary CV friction, and enters a verified employer workflow whose result can be measured through interview/hire outcomes.

Each individual feature may exist elsewhere. The application thesis is the integrated process designed for a specific Berlin labour-market access problem.

Do not claim that no competitor uses any one of these techniques.

## 6. Sustainability / social-impact case

### 6.1 Working sustainability definition for Klinnova

Klinnova's strongest sustainability contribution is social/economic rather than an environmental-product claim.

The platform aims to make legitimate employment access more efficient and less exclusionary for work-authorised people whose German level or conventional CV profile reduces visibility in mainstream recruitment.

### 6.2 SDG 8 alignment — Decent Work and Economic Growth

Potential contribution:

- improved access to legitimate employment opportunities;
- clearer job conditions and language expectations;
- reduced dependence on informal/opaque job-search channels;
- employer verification and moderation;
- explicit rejection of undeclared work;
- measurable interview/hire outcomes;
- helping local employers access labour supply that may be missed by conventional filters.

Do not claim guaranteed job creation or guaranteed integration outcomes.

### 6.3 SDG 9 alignment — Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

Potential contribution:

- digital infrastructure for a more structured local hiring workflow;
- explainable matching and reusable candidate/employer data structures;
- digitalisation of small/local employer recruitment processes;
- evidence-driven marketplace operations and outcome measurement.

### 6.4 Candidate dignity / non-paternalistic framing

The product should not describe candidates as people to be "rescued."

The operating principle is that candidates and employers both need clearer conditions to recognize legitimate mutual fit.

This is commercially stronger and avoids weakening the candidate's agency.

## 7. Berlin impact case

### 7.1 Berlin as the deliberate initial market

The M5 operating plan keeps Berlin as the only active market during the validation stage.

This creates a measurable location-specific implementation rather than claiming national reach before evidence exists.

### 7.2 International population context

F2A records official evidence that Berlin has a large international/migration-background population. These statistics support the relevance of an international labour-market access problem but must not be equated directly with Klinnova's addressable candidate market.

### 7.3 Labour-market matching relevance

F2A records current labour-market evidence showing that unemployment and employer labour demand can coexist and that German-language requirements materially affect labour-market integration.

This supports Klinnova's working thesis that part of the problem is not simply "lack of jobs" but friction in identifying jobs for which a candidate is actually suitable.

### 7.4 Cleaning/facility-services wedge

The cleaning-first strategy is a market-entry wedge, not Klinnova's permanent total market.

Reasons:

- structured operational requirements;
- comparatively lower language dependency in many tasks;
- recurring demand potential;
- substantial Berlin employer/business base;
- fit with the current product's availability/location/shift matching model;
- opportunity to measure real interview/hire conversion quickly without broad sector expansion.

### 7.5 Potential positive effects for Berlin

The application may reasonably argue for expected positive effects through:

- digitalisation of recruitment for small/local employers;
- improved labour-market participation among work-authorised residents;
- more efficient matching of existing local labour demand/supply;
- future recurring revenue/business growth based in Berlin;
- potential future employment/contractor demand as Klinnova scales;
- local specialist expenditure during the funded project where appropriate.

Any quantitative job-creation claim must come from the final staffing/financial plan, not from aspiration.

## 8. Market and growth potential connection

The impact case must remain connected to a commercial business model.

Klinnova is not planned as a grant-dependent social project. The current F2B recommendation is an employer-side freemium hybrid:

- core candidate participation remains free;
- basic employer entry remains low-friction;
- future employer revenue attaches to recurring recruiting value and optional services after willingness-to-pay is evidenced.

This supports a funding narrative in which:

1. the social benefit comes from lowering labour-market friction;
2. the paying customer is primarily the employer side;
3. the business can pursue market income rather than depend primarily on subsidies;
4. the grant accelerates market establishment and evidence-driven refinement rather than becoming the permanent revenue model.

## 11. Claim discipline

### 11.1 Claims currently supportable

The Businessplan can state that Klinnova:

- is a digital labour-market platform focused initially on Berlin;
- targets work-authorised candidates for whom limited German can create recruitment friction;
- uses structured language/availability/location/employment requirements in matching;
- supports a non-CV-first pathway where the employer does not require a CV;
- has employer verification/moderation controls;
- has an existing working MVP;
- plans controlled Berlin beta validation with measurable employment outcomes;
- has a commercial employer-side monetisation thesis rather than relying on grants as permanent revenue.

### 11.2 Claims that require qualification

Use cautious wording for:

- "labour shortage" — sector/date/source dependent;
- "underserved market" — support with competitor and user evidence;
- "better matching" — currently a product hypothesis until live external cohort evidence exists;
- "reduces no-shows" — employer need/hypothesis, not yet proven outcome;
- "creates jobs" — expected effect only until actual attributable outcomes exist;
- "improves integration" — plausible intended effect, not guaranteed.

### 11.3 Claims not currently authorized

Do not claim:

- proven reduction in unemployment;
- guaranteed hires;
- proven superior conversion to Indeed/Stepstone/BA;
- AI innovation if the actual authoritative matcher remains deterministic;
- comprehensive legal/GDPR compliance;
- qualified legal approval where none has been obtained;
- environmental impact that has not been measured;
- nationwide impact while the active validation boundary remains Berlin.

## 12. Innovation risks and mitigation

### Risk A — perceived as another job board

Mitigation:

- demonstrate language compatibility and practical eligibility as structured workflow;
- show the non-CV-first access path;
- emphasize verification/outcome measurement;
- present cleaning as a validation wedge rather than a generic listing category.

### Risk B — weak technological novelty

Mitigation:

- do not overclaim deep-tech novelty;
- rely on digital/social/process innovation permitted by the programme;
- connect innovation to measurable labour-market outcomes and sustainable commercial operation.

### Risk C — impact argument appears charitable rather than commercial

Mitigation:

- preserve employer-side monetisation;
- show market/growth potential;
- position inclusion as improved market efficiency, not subsidy dependence;
- connect impact KPIs to commercial funnel metrics.

### Risk D — impact becomes unmeasurable

Mitigation:

- use existing M5 outcome semantics;
- define cohort gates;
- count completed interviews/hire outcomes conservatively;
- do not substitute registrations for employment outcomes.

## 13. Recommended Businessplan F5 wording direction

Working English direction:

> Klinnova is a Berlin-based digital employment platform designed to reduce avoidable matching friction between employers and work-authorised jobseekers whose German level or conventional CV profile limits their visibility in standard recruitment processes. Rather than treating language accessibility as a translation problem alone, Klinnova makes language requirements, availability, location, employment type and other practical eligibility factors explicit parts of the vacancy and matching workflow. The platform combines explainable matching, a non-CV-first candidate path where appropriate, employer verification and measurable interview/hire outcomes. The initial Berlin market-entry phase focuses on cleaning and closely related low-language-barrier work to validate the model in a controlled segment before broader expansion.

Working German direction:

> Klinnova ist eine in Berlin aufgebaute digitale Beschäftigungsplattform, die vermeidbare Matching-Hürden zwischen Arbeitgebern und arbeitsberechtigten Arbeitssuchenden reduzieren soll, deren Deutschkenntnisse oder konventionelles Lebenslaufprofil ihre Sichtbarkeit in üblichen Recruiting-Prozessen einschränken. Sprachliche Anforderungen werden dabei nicht nur als Übersetzungsfrage behandelt, sondern gemeinsam mit Verfügbarkeit, Standort, Beschäftigungsart und weiteren praktischen Eignungskriterien strukturiert in den Stellen- und Matchingprozess integriert. Klinnova verbindet nachvollziehbares Matching, einen nicht zwingend lebenslaufbasierten Bewerbungsweg, Arbeitgeberverifizierung und die Messung tatsächlicher Interview- und Einstellungsergebnisse. Der initiale Markteintritt in Berlin konzentriert sich auf Reinigung und eng angrenzende Tätigkeiten mit vergleichsweise niedrigen Sprachbarrieren, um das Modell zunächst in einem kontrollierten Segment zu validieren.

These are working drafts, not final application/legal wording.
