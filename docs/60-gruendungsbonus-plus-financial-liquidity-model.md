## 1. Purpose

Create the financial-planning architecture required for the GründungsBONUS Plus Businessplan without inventing the Founder’s available cash, borrowing capacity, private living-cost contribution, or final grant budget.

Current official IBT guidance requires:

- a financial and liquidity plan covering at least three years;
- Year 1 shown monthly;
- later years may be shown quarterly;
- the GründungsBONUS Plus grant cash inflow to be reflected over the two-year approval period in up to four tranches.

The current programme page states that eligible expenditure must arise within two years from application receipt and that the grant can cover 50% of eligible costs up to EUR 50,000.

This document therefore separates:

1. the operating/revenue model;
2. the funded-project cost envelope;
3. the grant reimbursement cash-flow layer; and
4. Founder/co-financing inputs that remain unresolved.

## 2. Core modelling principles

### 2.1 No historical Klinnova costs

Historical MVP development, historical hosting/subscriptions, already-incurred legal/business costs, already-paid domain costs, and other pre-application expenditure are excluded from the proposed funded-project model.

### 2.2 Cohort A remains free

The Founder-approved Cohort A commercial position remains unchanged:

- candidates free;
- employers free;
- no subscription;
- no placement fee;
- no commission;
- no other paid Cohort A service.

Any revenue appearing in this F3 model begins only after the private-beta validation phase and is a forecasting assumption, not a change to current product pricing.

### 2.3 Candidate core access remains free in the working model

The F2B recommendation is preserved: core candidate onboarding, matching and application remain free in the long-term base model.

### 2.4 Employer revenue uses a freemium-hybrid base case

For modelling only, the F2B base range of EUR 49–59/month per paying employer is used as the central recurring-revenue hypothesis after evidence supports monetisation.

No public price is adopted by this document.

### 2.5 Optional one-off revenue is excluded from the base case

Future vacancy boosts, one-off recruiting services, institutional revenue, success fees or qualified-application fees are excluded from the base forecast until evidence exists. This keeps the base case conservative and avoids counting hypothetical secondary products twice.

## 3. Modelling horizon

Working horizon:

- **Year 1:** months 1–12 from funded-project start;
- **Year 2:** months 13–24;

This rolling-year approach is useful for modelling the operating phase. The final application workbook may need calendar-year presentation depending on IBT’s form/template; this must be reconciled before submission.

The GründungsBONUS Plus funded-cost/reimbursement period is modelled only across Years 1–2. Year 3 demonstrates post-grant operating sustainability.

## 7. Liquidity architecture

### 7.1 Why grant amount is not the same as available cash

The programme is reimbursement-oriented. The company must be capable of carrying:

- its own share of eligible expenditure;
- the period between paying invoices/payroll and receiving reimbursement;
- non-eligible operating expenditure;
- ordinary business cash needs outside the funded project.

A nominal EUR 50,000 maximum grant therefore does not mean the business needs only EUR 50,000 of liquidity.

### 7.2 Grant tranches

Current IBT guidance states the requested grant should be reflected in the liquidity plan across the two-year approval period in **up to four tranches**.

The final liquidity model should therefore contain explicit rows for:

- grant receivable / requested amount;
- expected tranche 1;
- expected tranche 2;
- expected tranche 3;
- expected tranche 4;
- timing buffer between expenditure and reimbursement.

Do not assume exact tranche dates until IBT process timing is clarified and the application schedule is fixed.

### 7.3 Minimum cash-balance rule

The final model should define a minimum operating cash buffer rather than allowing the forecast balance to approach zero.

A suitable buffer cannot be fixed responsibly until the Founder states:

- available business cash/equity contribution;
- maximum additional monthly contribution from employment income, if any;
- acceptable debt/credit use, if any;
- minimum private financial safety boundary;
- whether external financing is realistically available.

## 10. Break-even logic

The model should calculate two break-even views:

### Operating break-even

Monthly recurring employer revenue covers recurring operating expenses excluding grant reimbursement.

Formula:

`required paying employers = recurring monthly operating costs / employer ARPA`

### Full-company cash break-even

All normal business cash outflows, including personnel and non-grant operating costs, are covered by operating cash inflow without Founder injections or grant receipts.

No break-even employer count is recorded yet because the recurring monthly cost base is not yet sufficiently evidenced.
