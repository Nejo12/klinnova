## 1. Purpose

Determine which post-beta commercial model is most defensible for Klinnova given:

1. the current product and employer/candidate workflow;
2. the cleaning-first Berlin market-entry strategy;
3. the founder-approved Cohort A pricing position (free for candidates and employers; no subscription, placement fee or commission during Cohort A);
4. current competitor monetisation patterns; and
5. the need for credible 3-year financial assumptions in the GründungsBONUS Plus Businessplan.

This is a commercial-model decision analysis, not a final price list.

## 2. Governing constraints

### 2.1 Cohort A remains free

The current founder-approved private-beta commercial position remains unchanged:

- candidates: free;
- employers: free;
- subscriptions: none;
- placement/commission fees: none;
- no other paid Cohort A service.

This free beta is deliberate validation policy. It must not be retrospectively described as a permanent pricing commitment.

### 2.2 Candidate access should remain free as the default long-term direction

Klinnova's value proposition depends on reducing access friction for jobseekers whose German level or conventional CV profile already creates barriers. Charging candidates for access, matching or applications would weaken that proposition and could distort the social-impact case.

**Working recommendation:** core candidate access, onboarding, matching and application remain free.

This is a commercial recommendation, not a legal conclusion or permanent founder decision.

### 2.3 Employers already have free/low-cost publication alternatives

Current primary-source competitor evidence shows that employers can already publish jobs through strong alternatives at low or zero entry cost:

- Bundesagentur für Arbeit: job publication is explicitly free, with or without a placement mandate: https://web.arbeitsagentur.de/vermittlung/ag-stea-ui/pc/stellenmeldung
- Indeed Germany: up to three free job ads per employer per month (subject to restrictions/quality rules); paid Sponsored Jobs primarily buy visibility/matching: https://de.indeed.com/recruiting/preise
- Kleinanzeigen: from May 2026, commercial Jobs listings have zero free quota and are priced at roughly EUR 12.99–14.99 per additional/new job ad depending on conditions: https://themen.kleinanzeigen.de/gebuehren-kleinanzeigen-gewerbliche-nutzer/
- Stepstone: employers can create/manage ads for free in Stepstone Recruit but pay for Stepstone/partner-network distribution; All Jobs Starter advertises recurring packages from EUR 249/month: https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/kostenlose-stellenanzeige-schalten/ and https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/all-jobs-starter-angebot/

**Commercial consequence:** "pay us to post another vacancy" is not strong enough as Klinnova's primary monetisation thesis.

Klinnova must monetize differentiated employer value — better access to relevant candidates, reduced mismatch, controlled recruiting workflow, trust/verification, recurring hiring efficiency, or additional reach — not the mere existence of a listing form.

## 3. Competitor monetisation archetypes

### 3.1 Bundesagentur für Arbeit — public/free infrastructure

Model:

- free vacancy publication;
- optional free placement support;
- public labour-market service rather than commercial SaaS.

Implication for Klinnova:

- basic job posting cannot be defended as scarce value by itself;
- Klinnova must compete on workflow, target-group access, usability, relevance and service quality rather than price against BA.

### 3.2 Indeed — freemium + sponsored reach/matching

Current model evidence:

- limited free job ads;
- Sponsored Jobs with flexible budgets;
- Premium Sponsored Jobs with higher visibility, advanced matching, branding and candidate invitations;
- separate sourcing/subscription capabilities.

Source: https://de.indeed.com/recruiting/preise

Implication:

- free core supply can coexist with paid employer-side acceleration;
- monetisation can attach to visibility, reach, sourcing and recruiting efficiency rather than basic access.

### 3.3 Stepstone — paid distribution + recurring recruiting subscription

Current model evidence:

- free creation/management in Stepstone Recruit;
- paid publication/distribution;
- recurring All Jobs packages;
- Starter offer from EUR 249/month for ongoing job visibility.

Sources:

- https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/kostenlose-stellenanzeige-schalten/
- https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/all-jobs-starter-angebot/

Implication:

- recurring employer subscriptions are accepted in the market when they provide continuous recruiting value;
- however, Stepstone's reach and brand mean its absolute pricing is not an appropriate early Klinnova benchmark.

### 3.4 Kleinanzeigen — low-cost per-listing monetisation

Current model evidence:

- Jobs advertising for commercial users uses per-ad fees in the low tens of euros;
- monetisation is simple and transactional.

Source: https://themen.kleinanzeigen.de/gebuehren-kleinanzeigen-gewerbliche-nutzer/

Implication:

- per-vacancy pricing is operationally simple but faces strong price anchoring;
- a Klinnova per-ad fee would need differentiated candidate access or service value to justify itself.

### 3.5 Zenjob — usage/fulfilled-work monetisation

Current model evidence:

- no monthly fee or minimum purchase for short-term staffing;
- companies pay for hours actually worked;
- Zenjob operates as a digital personnel service/staffing model, including temporary employment and longer-term student staffing.

Source: https://www.zenjob.com/de/personal/

Implication:

- charging close to delivered labour value can reduce employer entry friction;
- but Zenjob's staffing/employee-leasing operating model is materially different from Klinnova's intended job marketplace and should not be copied structurally.

### 3.6 Helpling — transaction/service marketplace monetisation

Current model evidence:

- Helpling connects customers with self-employed service providers rather than operating a conventional employer/jobseeker hiring platform;
- customers pay a per-service platform fee (currently EUR 2.90 per completed service in the cited support material);
- provider-side registration/other fees may apply depending on current terms/location;
- Helpling's terms also describe platform mediation/commission mechanics.

Sources:

- https://help.helpling.de/de/articles/250519-wie-hoch-ist-die-servicegebuhr
- https://www.helpling.de/gebuehren-dienstleister_ed/
- https://www.helpling.de/agb-reinigungskraft/

Implication:

- Helpling demonstrates that cleaning demand can support transaction-linked marketplace revenue;
- however, its self-employed service-provider relationship is explicitly different from Klinnova's legitimate-employment focus and must not be used as a direct operating template.

## 4. Commercial models considered for Klinnova

### Model A — paid vacancy only

Employer pays a fixed amount whenever a vacancy is published.

**Advantages**

- simple to understand;
- simple billing trigger;
- revenue scales with vacancy volume;
- usable by occasional private/small employers without subscription commitment.

**Weaknesses**

- directly competes with free BA and free/low-cost alternatives;
- creates friction before Klinnova has proven applicant quality;
- discourages early marketplace supply;
- low willingness-to-pay risk for small cleaning employers;
- does not reward Klinnova for deeper recruiting value.

**F2B classification:** useful later as an optional one-off product, but **not recommended as the primary commercial model**.

### Model B — employer subscription only

Employer pays monthly/annual recurring fee for access to Klinnova.

Potential paid value could include multiple active vacancies, employer workflow tools, richer applicant management, sourcing, analytics, priority support or advanced matching features as they are evidenced and built.

**Advantages**

- predictable recurring revenue;
- easier financial modelling;
- aligns with employers with recurring demand;
- cleaning/property/facility businesses may have recurring hiring needs.

**Weaknesses**

- difficult to sell before sufficient candidate liquidity exists;
- occasional/private employers do not fit well;
- subscription churn can be high if hiring demand is seasonal;
- current Cohort A product does not yet justify a broad SaaS bundle.

**F2B classification:** **strong medium-term component**, but too early as the sole model.

### Model C — placement/success fee

Employer pays only after a defined successful hiring outcome.

**Advantages**

- low employer entry friction;
- strong perceived alignment with outcomes;
- potentially higher revenue per successful hire;
- directly matches Klinnova's outcome-measurement capability.

**Weaknesses**

- delayed and volatile revenue;
- hire attribution/disputes can be operationally difficult;
- incentives may encourage off-platform closure/avoidance;
- legal/contractual classification and employer terms require careful qualified review before adoption;
- private-household and low-wage roles may not support high placement fees;
- can make Klinnova look closer to an agency than the current product strategy intends.

**F2B classification:** **do not use as the initial post-beta default**. Revisit only after real hire data, willingness-to-pay evidence and legal/commercial review.

### Model D — pay per qualified application/interview

Employer pays when a defined applicant or interview-quality event occurs.

**Advantages**

- monetises closer to delivered value than a listing fee;
- lower upfront friction;
- potentially fits Klinnova's structured matching/outcome system.

**Weaknesses**

- defining "qualified" fairly is difficult;
- employer disputes likely;
- risks incentivising application volume rather than hiring quality;
- billing logic becomes product-critical;
- may distort matching/marketplace incentives.

**F2B classification:** **defer**. Interesting experiment only after beta evidence supports a trustworthy definition.

### Model E — freemium employer platform

Basic employer participation remains free within defined limits; paid plans/features attach to recurring recruiting value.

Illustrative structure (not final pricing):

**Basic / free**

- verified employer profile;
- limited number of active vacancies;
- receive and review applications;
- standard matching/distribution;
- core status/outcome workflow.

**Paid employer tier**

Potentially, once justified by evidence:

- more active vacancies;
- reusable vacancy templates;
- priority/boosted visibility;
- candidate sourcing/invitations where consent and product design allow;
- enhanced applicant workflow;
- employer/team capabilities;
- analytics/outcome reporting;
- faster support or assisted recruiting;
- integrations later if real customers require them.

**Advantages**

- minimizes supply-side entry friction;
- preserves marketplace growth;
- creates a natural path from beta to revenue;
- recurring-demand employers self-select into paid value;
- aligns with successful Indeed/Stepstone freemium/paid-recruiting patterns without copying them.

**Weaknesses**

- requires enough differentiated paid functionality;
- free/paid boundary must be designed carefully;
- early revenue may be modest;
- cannot be financially modelled as mature SaaS immediately.

**F2B classification:** **recommended core model direction**.

### Model F — hybrid freemium + one-off employer purchases

Combine free basic access with two paid paths:

1. recurring employer plan for organizations with ongoing recruitment; and
2. one-off paid options for occasional employers who do not want a subscription.

Possible future one-off products include:

- vacancy boost/priority distribution;
- extra active vacancy slot;
- founder-assisted hiring/onboarding package where operationally sustainable;
- time-limited recruiting campaign.

**Advantages**

- fits both recurring cleaning businesses and occasional/private employers;
- reduces forced subscription friction;
- creates multiple revenue paths without charging candidates;
- allows product/value evidence to determine which paid feature wins.

**Weaknesses**

- more pricing complexity than a single model;
- requires disciplined product scope to avoid building paid features speculatively.

**F2B classification:** **recommended commercial architecture**, implemented only after evidence.

### Model G — institutional / partner-funded access

Potential future revenue from municipalities, NGOs, integration/employment programs, language schools, workforce programs, large employers or associations that pay for access/outcomes/support while candidates remain free.

**Advantages**

- aligns with social-impact proposition;
- can subsidize candidate access;
- may provide trusted acquisition channels.

**Weaknesses**

- long B2B/public-sector sales cycles;
- procurement complexity;
- dependence on grants/contracts;
- risk of distracting from core marketplace proof.

**F2B classification:** **future secondary channel**, not the initial commercial engine.

## 5. Recommended commercial architecture

### Recommendation: employer-side freemium hybrid

The strongest current Businessplan direction is:

> **Candidates use Klinnova free of charge. Employers can enter and prove value through a limited free core offering. Klinnova monetises employers later through recurring recruiting plans and optional one-off visibility/workflow services once real market evidence demonstrates willingness to pay.**

This model is preferable now because:

1. employer acquisition is difficult enough without an upfront posting fee;
2. free/public competitors make basic listing access a commodity;
3. Klinnova first needs marketplace liquidity and proof of application/interview quality;
4. recurring cleaning/property employers offer a plausible subscription segment;
5. occasional/private employers need a non-subscription route;
6. candidates should not bear the monetisation burden;
7. it gives the Businessplan a credible route to recurring revenue without pretending final pricing is already validated.

## 6. Recommended commercial phases

### Phase 0 — Cohort A: proof, free

Current approved state.

- candidates free;
- employers free;
- no payment implementation;
- measure employer acquisition, vacancy supply, applications, completed interviews, hires and support burden;
- explicitly ask participating employers about willingness to pay only as research, after value has been experienced.

**No change authorized by this document.**

### Phase 1 — validation: pricing research, still low friction

After sufficient employer activity:

- conduct structured willingness-to-pay interviews;
- test which value employers actually pay for: reach, faster filling, workflow, sourcing, multiple vacancies, assistance or outcome quality;
- measure recurring vs occasional hiring frequency;
- segment organization employers from private/occasional employers;
- do not build payments until a pricing hypothesis survives evidence.

### Phase 2 — first monetisation experiment

Working hypothesis for later Founder decision:

- keep a free Basic employer tier;
- introduce one paid recurring organization tier;
- optionally introduce one simple one-off purchase for occasional employers;
- avoid placement commission as the first monetisation experiment;
- keep candidate core access free.

### Phase 3 — mature model

Only after proven demand:

- refine plan tiers;
- consider annual plans;
- add advanced sourcing/workflow/analytics only where used;
- evaluate institutional partnerships;
- reconsider success-based fees only if evidence and legal review support them.

## 7. Pricing hypotheses for F3 financial modelling

The following are **scenario assumptions only**, not approved public prices.

F3 requires numbers, but F2B does not yet have willingness-to-pay evidence. Therefore financial modelling should use ranges/scenarios rather than one asserted price.

### Employer subscription scenario range

Use a conservative modelling range such as:

- low case: EUR 29/month per paying organization;
- base case: EUR 49–59/month;
- higher case: EUR 79–99/month where recurring recruiting value is proven.

These values are hypotheses chosen to remain materially below large-board recurring products such as Stepstone's advertised EUR 249/month starter level while still representing meaningful B2B SaaS revenue. They are **not competitor-equivalent prices and not a pricing decision**.

### One-off purchase scenario range

Potential modelling range for an eventual boost/extra vacancy/campaign product:

- EUR 15–39 per purchase.

This range reflects the existence of low-cost per-ad competitors such as Kleinanzeigen while allowing Klinnova to price differentiated recruiting value rather than merely copying listing fees.

Again: **scenario only**.

### Paying-conversion assumptions

F3 should avoid aggressive SaaS conversion assumptions. Suggested scenario architecture:

- free employer base remains larger than paying base;
- paying conversion begins only after Cohort/Beta validation;
- recurring-demand organization employers are the primary paying cohort;
- private/occasional employers primarily use free or one-off products;
- no candidate revenue;
- no placement-fee revenue in the base case.

Exact percentages must be defined in F3 as explicit assumptions, not presented as observed data.

## 8. Why success fees are not recommended in the base Businessplan

A success fee can look attractive because it links payment to hiring. It is not the best base model for the current funding application because:

- no real Cohort A hire-conversion rate exists yet;
- revenue timing would be highly uncertain;
- employer circumvention/attribution becomes a material operating problem;
- employment/intermediation legal terms would require additional review;
- it complicates the financial plan unnecessarily;
- the existing product is designed as a marketplace/workflow platform, not a full recruiting agency.

The Businessplan can mention success-based monetisation as a **future tested option**, but it should not underpin the base 3-year financial case at this stage.

## 9. Businessplan wording direction

Recommended English working formulation:

> Klinnova follows an employer-funded freemium model. Jobseekers use the core platform free of charge. During the initial private beta, employers also participate free of charge so that product-market fit and hiring outcomes can be validated without pricing friction. After validation, Klinnova plans to retain a limited free employer offering while monetising recurring hiring needs through paid employer plans and optional one-off recruiting services. The exact price points and paid feature boundary will be validated with participating employers before commercial rollout. The financial plan therefore models conservative pricing scenarios rather than treating untested prices as established market facts.

Recommended German working formulation:

> Klinnova verfolgt perspektivisch ein arbeitgeberfinanziertes Freemium-Modell. Für Arbeitssuchende bleibt die Kernnutzung der Plattform kostenfrei. Während der ersten privaten Beta-Phase nehmen auch Arbeitgeber kostenfrei teil, damit Produkt-Markt-Fit und tatsächliche Vermittlungsergebnisse ohne Preisbarriere validiert werden können. Nach erfolgreicher Validierung soll ein begrenztes kostenloses Arbeitgeberangebot bestehen bleiben. Wiederkehrender Rekrutierungsbedarf soll über kostenpflichtige Arbeitgeberpakete und optionale einmalige Recruiting-Leistungen monetarisiert werden. Die konkreten Preise und Leistungsgrenzen werden vor dem kommerziellen Rollout anhand realer Arbeitgeberdaten und Zahlungsbereitschaft validiert. Der Finanzplan arbeitet deshalb mit konservativen Szenarien und nicht mit bereits als bewiesen dargestellten Preisen.

Do not use this wording as a public pricing promise before Founder approval.

## 10. Evidence to collect during beta before pricing implementation

For every participating employer, collect lightweight commercial evidence after they have experienced real value:

- hiring frequency per quarter/year;
- number of vacancies likely to be posted;
- current channels used;
- current spend on job advertising/recruiting;
- biggest recruiting cost/friction today;
- which Klinnova outcome had value (relevant applicant, reduced mismatch, interview, hire, workflow convenience, language fit);
- whether they would pay for continued use;
- preferred payment shape: per vacancy / monthly / annual / success-based / no preference;
- acceptable price range, asked neutrally rather than leading with one number;
- reason for refusal if unwilling to pay.

This becomes the evidence gate before final pricing implementation.

## 11. Commercial decision gates

### Gate C1 — no pricing implementation before value proof

Do not build billing/subscriptions merely because the Businessplan needs revenue assumptions.

### Gate C2 — pricing research after real employer use

Commercial interviews should follow experienced product value, not precede Cohort A as sales pressure.

### Gate C3 — one primary paid experiment

When monetisation begins, test one coherent paid proposition first rather than subscriptions + boosts + success fees + packages simultaneously.

### Gate C4 — candidate core remains free in base case

Any future candidate-paid feature would require a separate Founder decision and social-impact review.

### Gate C5 — legal review before success/placement fees

Do not introduce employer placement/commission mechanics without appropriate commercial/legal review of the exact model and terms.

## 12. F2B verdict

**F2B — COMPLETE.**

Recommended Businessplan base model:

- **candidate side:** free core access;
- **employer side:** freemium;
- **primary future revenue:** recurring employer plan for organizations with recurring hiring demand;
- **secondary future revenue:** simple one-off employer purchases/services for occasional demand;
- **placement/success fee:** not included in the base case; future option only;
- **institutional partnerships:** secondary longer-term option;
- **Cohort A:** remains fully free under existing Founder decision.

## 13. Consequence for F3

F3 should now model three scenarios rather than one false-precision forecast:

1. **Conservative** — slow employer conversion, low subscription price, limited one-off revenue;
2. **Base** — moderate organization conversion, mid-range employer plan, modest one-off revenue;
3. **Upside** — stronger recurring-employer conversion and higher validated plan value.

All assumptions must be labelled explicitly as planning assumptions until real beta evidence exists.
